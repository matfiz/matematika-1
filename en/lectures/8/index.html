Differentiation rules

What this lecture does

The derivative definition gives rules for building derivatives from simpler ones. We prove the arithmetic, chain and inverse rules, keeping the conditions needed for division and composition. The standard limits then give an elementary derivative table. Every formula is used on a domain where its hypotheses hold.

Summary

  • Theorem 8.1: sums and products of differentiable functions are differentiable. A quotient also requires a nonzero denominator.
  • Theorem 8.2: (g∘f)′(c)=g′(f(c))f′(c)(g\circ f)'(c)=g'(f(c))f'(c)(g∘f)′(c)=g′(f(c))f′(c) when both pointwise derivatives exist and the composition is defined near ccc.
  • Standard limits prove the exponential, logarithmic, sine and cosine derivatives. The arithmetic and chain rules give the power and tangent formulas.
  • Theorem 8.3: a continuous strictly monotone interval function has inverse derivative 1/f′(c)1/f'(c)1/f′(c) at the image of ccc if f′(c)≠0f'(c)\ne0f′(c)=0. This gives the inverse trigonometric derivatives on their stated interiors.
  • Hyperbolic derivatives follow from their exponential definitions. Differentiability alone does not imply continuity of the derivative.
